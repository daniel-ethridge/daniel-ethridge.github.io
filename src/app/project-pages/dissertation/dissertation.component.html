Dissertation Research

Overlooked Voices and Overlooked Memories:
A Mixed-Methods Investigation of the Roles of Vocals on Musically Induced Nostalgia

In May 2026, I successfully proposed my research plan for my PhD dissertation research which I will be conducting and writing up from August 2026 through mid February 2027. This will ideally lead to an April 2027 defense.

Abstract

Current musically induced emotion research aims to uncover how music influences affective changes in us. This research provides a foundation for applications to fields such as music therapy, film, general well-being, and marketing. Despite the broad and important applications, prior research largely focuses on a small subset of human emotions like happiness, sadness, and fear. Notably, this list excludes nostalgia, a complex emotion that is linked to our identities and memories and that has researched applications to mood and emotion regulation. Studies routinely utilize measurement techniques like surveys and questionnaires with closed-ended questions and forced-choice response paradigms that do not allow for participants to speak their nuanced, individual truth about the feelings and emotions that music induces in them. Furthermore, the voice, despite being an information-rich signal important to music emotion induction, is not adequately studied in terms of its roles in musically induced nostalgia. These methodological weaknesses across prior work have stunted our understanding of musically induced nostalgia and has limited our grasp on the roles that vocals play in this process. In this dissertation proposal, I provide a brief overview of theories governing musically induced emotion as well as prior work in the field with special attention paid to nostalgia. Next, I discuss my methodological approach to this research, post-positivism, followed by my completed work. Finally, I discuss plans for two projects that implement a mixed-methods research design in order to approach the problems from different angles. The first project utilizes text mining methods to analyze song lyrics in order to better understand the role of vocals in musically induced nostalgia. The second project utilizes both quantitative methods and a semi-structured interview approach to study the role of nostalgia in music-driven recollections of our strongest and most poignant memories. This work will result in empirical and methodological contributions for this field that will deepen our understanding of how nostalgic music affects us emotionally, the specific roles of vocals in that process, and how different intensities of nostalgia may impact mood and emotion regulation through listening to nostalgic music.
